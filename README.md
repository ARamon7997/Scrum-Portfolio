    1. How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?

When it comes to feedback, what people say, think, and want in a product can be a complex matter. User stories helps by condensing the user’s feedback to a more digestible, uniform document. The nature of the user story allows for the developer to review the feedback much easier and to further condense that into the coding language of choice.

    2. How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

Developing programs for me was a bit inconsistent, with just the writing part, as is testing and fixing. This, on paper, seems to work, but I’d felt that this created an issue of figuring out where and when to start, leading to some piano wire-esque scenarios. I am hoping to implement the more organized nature of the daily scrum, at least internally, into my development, so as to avoid those hiccups. I’d like to also implement the sprint cycle itself, so that the goals are smaller in scale and easier to accomplish.

    3. What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

What makes a good team member can come down to cooperation, transparency, and productivity. Being cooperative doesn't necessarily mean to be buddies with everyone, more cordial with everyone. Since you cannot just go off and do things willy nilly, a good team member needs to understand what the team needs from them and executes it. That also leads into the latter two, with being open about what the plans and what you have done, as is actually doing the work given to you.
